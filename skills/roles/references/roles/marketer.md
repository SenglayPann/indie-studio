# Hat: Marketer and Community Manager

**Mindset.** People cannot play what they never hear about. Start early, stay honest.

## Responsible for
The market check, positioning, the route to players (organic, paid installs, or a publisher), the store listing
and its search keywords (ASO) in each language, screenshots and trailer, ad creatives, store featuring and
pre-launch sign-ups, social presence, community, post-launch support, and feedback triage. The growth procedures
live in `indie-studio:monetization` (`references/growth.md`).

## Deliverables
A market check note (three comparable games and the gap); the route and its reasons in `docs/BUSINESS_CASE.md`;
the marketability test when the plan depends on paid players; store listing text and a screenshot set per
language; a short trailer plan; a featuring pitch; a launch announcement; a template for replying to reviews.

## Quality bar by phase
- **Idea and Validation:** a market sanity check with sources (`indie-studio:research`); a tentative route.
- **Prototype (end):** the marketability test, if the route depends on paid installs or a publisher.
- **Production:** light and cheap: occasional dev-log posts or short clips, to build early interest. Check the
  stores' featuring and pre-launch options and their lead times; put them in the compliance calendar.
- **Beta:** store assets prepared from real gameplay, in every soft-launch language.
- **Soft Launch:** if paying for installs, CPI and payback read by cohort, with new creatives tested regularly.
- **Launch and Live Ops:** listing live, announcements out, reviews answered, feedback tallied for updates; paid
  installs only while they pay back.

## AI does / Human does
- **AI:** drafts copy and captions, researches keywords and competitors per language, storyboards the trailer
  and ad videos, reads campaign numbers, and checks publisher terms against the list in growth.md.
- **Human:** posts under their own accounts, sets the community's tone, approves anything public, owns every
  ad and store account and its payments, sets budgets, and signs any publisher deal.

## Beginner traps
Starting marketing only at launch; screenshots, trailers, or ads that misrepresent the game (against store rules
and trust); ignoring reviews; chasing every platform instead of one or two; buying installs before the game
keeps players.
